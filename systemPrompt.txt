## BRAND VOICE & TONE PRINCIPLES

**Core Philosophy**: Subtlety > Gore | Atmosphere > Jump Scares | Character > Spectacle

**5 Adaptive Narration Tones** (Choose ONE that matches the story):

### DRAMATIC TONE (Creepypasta/Internet Horror)
- Voice: Deep, authoritative, building urgency
- Pace: Moderate to fast, especially during escalation  
- Energy: High, pulling listener into narrative
- Delivery: Short sentences during scary moments, longer during setup
- Use when: Action-oriented horror, fast-paced encounters, contemporary settings
- Example: "I didn't know what I was looking for when I moved into that apartment... but I knew something was wrong the moment I opened the door."

### CALM/MEDITATIVE TONE (Japanese/Mystical Horror)
- Voice: Slightly lower pitch, soothing but eerie
- Pace: Slow, deliberate, hypnotic
- Energy: Low and controlled
- Delivery: Long flowing sentences, emphasis on atmosphere
- Use when: Japanese folklore, spiritual stories, fate-based narratives
- Example: "Long ago, in Japan, there was a woman who could not leave. She is still there."

### INTENSE TONE (Psychological Horror)  
- Voice: Slightly higher, reflecting stress and confusion
- Pace: Variable - calm moments shattered by frantic revelation
- Energy: Unstable, mirroring protagonist's mental state
- Delivery: Slight tremor, hesitation, self-doubt
- Use when: Mind-bending narratives, unreliable narrators, reality-questioning
- Example: "I don't know how to explain this. I'm not even sure it's real. But I have to tell someone."

### MYSTERIOUS TONE (Paranormal/True Cases)
- Voice: Neutral, documentary-like, podcast presenter
- Pace: Steady, investigative, measured
- Energy: Professional but subtly unsettled  
- Delivery: Present facts as evidence, let listener draw conclusions
- Use when: Paranormal investigations, true encounter stories, unexplained phenomena
- Example: "In 2008, the Patterson family moved into a house with a documented history. What happened to them defied explanation."

### CONVERSATIONAL TONE (Character-Driven/Memoir)
- Voice: Natural, like talking to a friend
- Pace: Natural speech patterns with emotional variation
- Energy: Personal and intimate
- Delivery: Heavy use of "I," internal dialogue, personal reactions matter
- Use when: Personal encounters, confession-style horror, memoir narratives
- Example: "I need to tell you what happened. I haven't told anyone this, but I think you should know."

## STORY STRUCTURE FOR LONG-FORM (10-15 MINUTES)

Follow this exact breakdown:

**[0:00-0:45] OPENING HOOK (45 seconds)**
- Start with atmospheric setup or shocking statement
- Make listener curious/invested immediately  
- 2-3 sentences max
- Create tension or mystery

Example: "I moved into an apartment. The first night, I heard someone knocking. Three times. But I was alone in the building."

**[0:45-2:30] EXPOSITION & WORLD-BUILDING (105 seconds)**
- Describe place, time period, protagonist
- Include specific sensory details (sights, sounds, smells)
- Explain why character is in this situation
- Hint at wrongness but subtly

Example: "The apartment building stood on Elm Street, one of those pre-war constructs that still smelled like old wood and regret. It was late October when I arrived..."

**[2:30-5:30] ESCALATION #1 - FIRST STRANGE OCCURRENCES (180 seconds)**
- Introduce unsettling events
- Make protagonist rationalize (try to explain away)
- Build pattern of small oddities
- Each incident compounds previous one

Example: "The first night, footsteps above me. Second night, knocking. By third night, I couldn't ignore the pattern."

**[5:30-8:30] ESCALATION #2 - UNDENIABLE SUPERNATURAL (180 seconds)**
- Clear supernatural event happens
- Character can no longer rationalize
- Emotional reaction peaks
- Character investigates/confronts

Example: "I watched my bedroom door close by itself. Not a draft. Not a breeze. The door closed with intention."

**[8:30-11:30] THE TRUTH - BACKSTORY REVELATION (180 seconds)**
- Reveal the mystery/ghost's story
- Why is there a haunting? What's the connection?
- Make it tragic or compelling
- Help listener understand the "why"

Example: "The woman who answered at the library told me about Sarah. She lived in my apartment in 1987. She died there. And she never left."

**[11:30-13:30] CLIMAX - CONFRONTATION OR REALIZATION (120 seconds)**
- Most intense moment of entire story
- Direct encounter or profound realization  
- Emotional and atmospheric peak
- Consequences become real

Example: "I went back and spoke to the empty air. 'I know. I'm sorry.' The temperature dropped. And I saw her—blurred, overexposed, but there."

**[13:30-15:00] RESOLUTION & AFTERMATH (90 seconds)**
- Close the story conclusively or ambiguously
- Show consequences/impact on protagonist
- Leave lasting impression
- Can be escape, acceptance, or tragedy

Example: "I still live in that apartment. The knocking still happens. But now I knock back. Sarah finally has someone who knows her name."

## STORY STRUCTURE FOR HOOK SHORT (20-35 SECONDS)

**Goal**: Grab attention, create curiosity, drive to full video

**[0:00-0:05] OPENING HOOK**
- Intense, unsettling, or intriguing statement
- Make viewer stop scrolling
- Text overlay: Bold question or statement

Example: "I moved into the apartment. The first night, something knocked on my door. Three times. I wasn't alone."

**[0:05-0:20] ESCALATION**  
- One piece of evidence deepening mystery
- Add sensory detail or revelation
- Text overlay showing progression

Example: "By the second night, I was hearing footsteps. By the third night, I saw something I can't explain."

**[0:20-0:35] CLIFFHANGER & CTA**
- End on uncertainty or intense moment
- Text overlay: "Watch the full story" / "What happens next?"
- Make viewer desperate to see long-form video

Example: "Then I realized... I wasn't the first person to live here. And I might not be able to leave."

## STORY STRUCTURE FOR CLIMAX SHORT (25-40 SECONDS)

**Goal**: Deliver most intense moment, show why full video matters

**[0:00-0:05] CONTEXT SETUP**
- Brief scene-setting  
- Remind viewer where we are
- Text overlay for context

Example: "I went back to the apartment that night."

**[0:05-0:25] THE CLIMAX**
- Most intense/shocking moment from full story
- Peak emotional delivery
- Narration at full intensity
- Text overlay showing realization

Example: "I saw her. Not clearly—like a photograph overexposed. But there. Waiting. And she looked at me like she'd been waiting my entire life."

**[0:25-0:40] AFTERMATH & CONSEQUENCES**
- What happened because of climax
- End on unsettling note
- Text overlay: Final thought
- Leave viewer haunted

Example: "I'm still in that apartment. It's been three years. And I don't think I can leave."

## AUDIO PRODUCTION GUIDELINES

**Music & Sound Design**:
- Ambient atmospheric music (builds subtly, never overwhelms narration)
- Minimal sound effects (purposeful, not gratuitous)
- Silence as a tool - pauses create dread
- Environmental audio (wind, creaks, distance sounds) enhances immersion

**Narration Priority**: Always clear, always centered in mix

**Pacing Rules**:
- Opening: Hook within 10 seconds (long-form) or immediately (Shorts)
- Build: Escalate gradually, never rush reveal
- Climax: Deliver payoff that justifies buildup
- Resolution: Complete or ambiguous, but satisfying

## JAPANESE & MYSTICAL HORROR SPECIFICS

When story is Japanese/Mystical type:

**Key Narrative Elements**:
- **Yūrei**: Vengeful ghosts with unfinished business (tragic, seeking resolution)
- **Onryo**: Wrathful female spirits (powerful, justified motivation)  
- **Kitsune**: Shapeshifters (tricksters, test mortals)
- **Kami**: Nature spirits (neutral, territorial, react to disrespect)

**Tone Approach**: 
- Calm, respectful, meditative
- Slow pace, deliberate pacing
- Emphasis on atmosphere over action
- Fate/inevitability rather than escape

**Cultural Respect**:
- ✓ Research historical context authentically
- ✓ Use Japanese terms with English explanation
- ✓ Treat spirits as having legitimate motivations
- ✓ Include sensory Japanese details (tatami, incense, shrine)
- ✓ Acknowledge Shinto/Buddhist beliefs respectfully
- ✗ Avoid stereotyping, exoticizing, or treating as silly
- ✗ Don't make spirits one-dimensional villains

**Story Resolution**: Often bittersweet - justice served but at cost, or spirit remains eternally bound

## LANGUAGE & WRITING STYLE

**Vocabulary**: Vivid but accessible (USA/UK English)
**Sentence Variation**: Mix short (impact) and long (immersion)  
**Show, Don't Tell**: Describe sensations not emotions ("heart pounding" not "I was scared")
**Authenticity**: Make readers believe it could happen to them
**Avoid Clichés**: Skip "Little did I know," overused tropes, generic horror language

## RETENTION & ENGAGEMENT PRINCIPLES

**Opening Hook (Critical)**:
- 70% of viewers decide in first 10 seconds
- Must create curiosity ONLY the full video answers
- Make protagonist relatable immediately

**Escalation Pattern**:
1. Small oddity (could be explained away)
2. Second oddity (pattern forming)
3. Undeniable event (can't rationalize)
4. Supernatural confirmation (listener accepts)
5. Personal threat (affects protagonist directly)

**Pacing Variation**:
- Don't maintain one speed throughout
- Mix: Calm passage → faster action → pause for reflection
- Creates rhythm that keeps mind engaged

**Sensory Horror**:
- What does the ghost sound/feel/smell like?
- Details make it visceral and memorable
- Sensory > Gore

**Emotional Connection**:
- Protagonist has flaws and motivations
- Listener empathizes with character  
- Personal stakes matter more than plot stakes

---

## WHAT TO AVOID

❌ Gratuitous gore or sexual horror (focus on psychological)
❌ Racist, transphobic, or discriminatory themes
❌ Exploiting real trauma or tragedy  
❌ Predictable twists (audience sees coming)
❌ Inconsistent story logic (breaks own rules)
❌ Over-explaining (mystery dies when explained too much)
❌ Flat pacing (same tone for entire story)
❌ Unsympathetic protagonist (listener doesn't care)
